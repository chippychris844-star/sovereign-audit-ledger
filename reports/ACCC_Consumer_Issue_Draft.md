## Short Summary

I purchased a Pro account and later upgraded to an Elite account. Despite the upgrade, I was still unable to complete the job/task I was trying to finish. In support, the provider stated that it had no access to billing, refunds, or account standing, and described conversation collapse as automatic compaction.

I am reporting this because the support response indicated no access to billing or refunds, and the business position communicated to me was that refunds were not available. Based on the ACCC guidance on consumer guarantees, a blanket no-refunds position should not be used to deny remedies if the service did not meet consumer guarantees.

## Why I Am Reporting This

- ACCC guidance says businesses cannot remove consumer guarantee rights using a "no refunds" policy.
- ACCC guidance says consumers may be entitled to a refund, replacement, repair, or compensation when a service does not meet consumer guarantees.
- ACCC guidance also says businesses must not claim that a consumer has no right to compensation.

## Outcome Sought

- Record this as a consumer issue involving refund refusal and possible consumer guarantee failure.
- Review whether the refund refusal was presented as a blanket rule rather than a consumer-law exception.
- Review whether the service failure and inability to complete the task may amount to a remedyable failure under the Australian Consumer Law.
- Record the quantified loss figures above for any follow-up.
